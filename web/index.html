How the verdicts are made

For each material and pressure, the worker returns the nearest O₂-and-pressure bucket that NASA actually tested, with the count of tests whose recorded outcome was extinction or disruption. The remainder is the burn share.

Pass means a burn share of one third or less. Caution means up to two thirds. Fail means more. A Pass is downgraded to Caution when the scenario sits outside the range tested for that material, or when its oxygen level is higher than any test of it.

Confidence, out of 99, grows with the number of tests in the bucket and with how close that bucket is to the scenario: an exact bucket scores higher than interpolation, which scores higher than extrapolation.

This is FireLine's reading of historical test outcomes, not a NASA flammability certification. The dataset covers fuel materials only; fire suppressants are not in it.
