AI-written Ranking briefing

Where NASA tested each fuel

Columns are oxygen, rows are pressure. Each filled cell is a set of conditions NASA actually tested: the bar shows how those tests ended and the number is how many there were. Empty cells were never tested, and pressures with no tests at all are left out. The solid outline is your nominal atmosphere and the dashed outline runs down to the depress floor.

Across the operational range

How the readings are made

Every test here was lit on purpose, so the question is how the flame ended. For each material and pressure, Phenox takes the nearest oxygen-and-pressure conditions NASA tested and counts the outcomes: went out on its own, kept burning, droplet broke apart, or no result recorded.

Only the first two are conclusive. Goes out means at least two thirds of them went out on their own, Mixed at least one third, and Keeps burning fewer. Goes out drops to Mixed outside the range tested for that material. No data means nothing close was tested (more than two bucket widths away) or no result was conclusive.

Each share has a 95% range that widens when few tests back it. This is a reading of historical results, not a NASA flammability certification, and Goes out never means safe to fly. All of the evidence is from microgravity.

Contribute data

      
Have a new NASA combustion CSV? Upload it and Phenox matches its columns the same way the PSI scrape was matched, adds it to Tiger Data, and folds it straight into the ranking and envelope above — the dataset grows as new experiments come in, instead of staying fixed. Uploads are marked community-contributed, unverified everywhere they're shown or cited, until someone reviews them; they never get presented with the same authority as the NASA PSI data.
